Research.

We study how climate hazards change daily life in cities, who can adapt, and how geospatial AI can support decisions about people and places.

GeoAI & Digital Twins for Urban Resilience

When floodwater reaches a neighborhood, which roads, homes, and services are at risk? We use satellite and street-view imagery, residents’ requests for help, and flood simulations to trace how a hazard moves through a city. During Hurricane Harvey, rescue requests revealed needs even in areas mapped as low risk.

We are extending this work to wildfire and extreme heat. We aim to build models that respect the physics of each hazard and show when one disruption can trigger another across roads, power grids, and other infrastructure.

Flood models and disaster response studies
  • FlowsDT

    A geospatial digital twin simulates how urban floods unfold in space and time.

  • VictimFinder

    A language model identifies requests for rescue in social media messages during a hurricane.

  • Street-level damage assessment

    Before-and-after imagery records damage at individual locations.

  • HazardWeave

    Our AI Tennessee THRIVE project brings hazard information together to support local emergency planning.

Urban Inequality & Segregation

Whose daily routines can change when temperatures rise? In Houston, mobile phone data showed that residents of higher-poverty neighborhoods had less flexibility to reduce daytime travel during extreme heat. We combine mobility records, street and satellite imagery, and census data to study how segregation shapes exposure and access to services beyond the home neighborhood.

We plan to examine how housing and transportation policies change exposure, and how the benefits and environmental costs of data centers and power grids are distributed across communities.

Studies of mobility, exposure, and access
  • Mobility during extreme heat

    Mobile phone records reveal differences in how Houston communities adjust their daily activities.

  • Winter Storm Uri and power outages

    Nighttime lights and travel patterns show how the 2021 Texas storm disrupted different communities.

  • Humanitarian needs during Shanghai’s lockdown

    Residents’ requests for food and medical care reveal needs that district-level resource counts miss.

Geospatial Foundation Models & Causal Intelligence

What does a model learn about a place beyond its coordinates? Our review of 1,122 studies examined how GeoAI models represent space. With GeoProbe, we test where language models encode geographic concepts. Our RegionFormer work builds distance decay and regional differences into forecasts.

We are exploring whether learned representations can help researchers compare similar places and estimate the effects of interventions when neighboring areas influence one another. We also plan to test how reliably models reason about space and transfer to new regions.

Foundation models and geographic reasoning
  • Measuring spatial explicitness in GeoAI

    A systematic review examines how geographic concepts enter model design and evaluation.

  • GeoProbe

    Layer-by-layer measurements test how language models represent distance decay and spatial autocorrelation.

  • SounDiT

    A diffusion model uses geographic context and sound to generate images of landscapes.

Responsible GeoAI for Decision Support

What evidence should an emergency manager see before acting on a model’s prediction? Our STEP framework brings security, trustworthiness, equity, and philanthropy into GIScience. In NORA, a spatial research agent, we put safeguards into the workflow so researchers can trace and reproduce an analysis.

We are developing ways to communicate uncertainty across places, explain predictions to the people using them, and flag decisions for human review when the evidence is thin.

Frameworks, uncertainty, and research tools
  • The STEP framework

    Four principles guide decisions about geographic data, methods, and whom the work serves.

  • NORA

    An autonomous research agent supports reproducible spatial data science from question to report.

  • Uncertainty in geolocating social media

    Different geolocation methods can change where disaster reports appear on a map.
